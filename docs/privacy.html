Privacy and house rules

Last updated 24 September 2026.

Planning an evening

You do not need an account to plan. Your answers and your last plan are kept in your own browser and nowhere else. To build a plan, the app looks up the place you give it with free public services: OpenStreetMap (places and map tiles), Open-Meteo (the forecast) and Wikipedia (photographs). They receive that location and your IP address, as any website you visit does, and nothing that identifies you. The page's fonts come from Google Fonts, which also sees your IP address.

The community

Sharing evenings and reacting to them needs an account. If you make one, Kindling stores:

  • Your email address, only to sign you in. It is never shown to anyone.
  • The name you choose and, if you give it, your home city. Both are public.
  • Evenings you share: the places, your notes, and the city. These are public and the planner uses them to suggest places to other people.
  • The evenings you love. Everyone sees how many loves an evening has; only you can see which ones you loved.
  • Reports you make, seen only by the people who run Kindling.

This is stored with Supabase, the database service Kindling runs on. There are no ads, no analytics, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Removing it

You can delete any evening you shared from the Community tab, and its loves go with it. To delete your account and everything attached to it, email yousseft20@outlook.com from the address you signed in with.

House rules

  • Share places you have actually been.
  • Nothing about other people: no names, photos or details of anyone else.
  • No ads, promotions or posts for a place you own or are paid by.
  • Kindling is for adults. You must be 18 or over to make an account.

Anything that breaks these can be reported. Three reports hide an evening until it has been reviewed, and posts or accounts that break the rules are removed.
